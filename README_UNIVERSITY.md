# University recruiting & new-grad channels

Companion to `README_WORKDAY.md`. That file tracks open-market postings.
This one tracks the **separate hiring channel** for early-career candidates:
university recruiting (UR), campus programs, and new-grad cohorts. These reqs
often never appear on LinkedIn, Indeed, or a company's main careers search.
They live on a distinct UR page, open on an academic calendar, and close fast.

---

## Read this before using the links

**Timing.** The fall cycle for 2027 starts is opening now (late Aug through
Nov). Salesforce Futureforce opens in August. Adobe opens just after Labor
Day. NVIDIA's windows can be brutally short: its Ignite program ran a 13-day
window in the 2026 cycle, posted Oct 6 and closed Oct 19. Check the pages
weekly in September and October, not monthly.

**Your eligibility is the live question.** You finished the MSCS in spring
2026. Most programs define "new grad" as graduating within a stated window,
commonly "Fall 2026 through Spring 2027" or "within the last 12 months."
Some of these you qualify for, some you don't, and it is not predictable from
the company. Read the eligibility line on every req before spending time on
it. Roughly speaking this is your last clean cycle for this channel.

**Design is thin here.** UR programs are overwhelmingly engineering, then
PM, then design. Design UR reqs exist at the companies with real design orgs
(Google, Adobe, Microsoft, IBM, Salesforce, Intuit, Meta) but there may be
five of them company-wide. Search each page for "design," "UX," "product
design," and "user experience" separately. Do not expect volume. Expect a
handful of very high-value shots where you are not competing against people
with four years of experience.

**Search terms that work on UR pages:** `design`, `UX`, `user experience`,
`product design`, `design technologist`, `UX engineer`, `associate designer`,
`rotational`.

Big 4 and the large consultancies hire early-career talent at massive volume
through structured campus programs, they have real digital and design arms,
and your PwC and EY background means you can speak their language and may
have live contacts. Campus hiring is how these firms are *built*. They are
also far less likely to filter you on years of design experience because the
program assumes you have none.

# Tier 5: worth adding, not on your Workday list

Companies and programs that specifically run design-friendly early-career
pipelines or are otherwise worth having on the radar.

Mad*Pow, DockYard, Upstatement, thoughtbot, Cantina are all local.
- **Rotational leadership programs** — the insurance and finance firms above often run 2-year rotational programs that include a design or digital rotation. Search each site for "rotational" or "development program."

# The aggregators, clarified

I overstated this in conversation and want to correct it.

## The GitHub repos: mostly not for you

The big new-grad GitHub repos are the canonical resource for this channel,
but they are **engineering-first**. The main one,
[SimplifyJobs/New-Grad-Positions](https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/New-Grad-Positions),
describes itself as a collection of full-time roles in SWE, quant, and PM.
Design is not a tracked category. You will find the occasional design req in
there but it is not a design resource.

**Still worth doing:** star and watch the repo, and skim it monthly. Two
reasons. First, a company appearing there with a 2027 new-grad SWE req almost
always has a UR page with other tracks on it, so the repo works as a
*discovery tool for which companies are actively running a cycle* even when
the specific listing is irrelevant. Second, the sibling repo
[SimplifyJobs/Summer2027-Internships](https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/Summer2027-Internships)
tracks the same for internships, which tells you when each company's cycle
opened.

There is also a community-built email alert service called SWEList that sends
updates for new roles in that repo. Same caveat: engineering-weighted.

## Simplify itself: more useful than the repo

[simplify.jobs](https://simplify.jobs) is the actual product behind those
repos and it does have a design track, including a curated
[entry-level UI/UX listing page](https://simplify.jobs/l/Entry-Level-UI-UX-Jobs).
It also has a browser extension that autofills applications, which is
directly useful given you have done 100+ of them. Make an account, set the
filters to product design / UX at 0-2 years, and turn on alerts.

## Others

- **[newgrad-jobs.com](https://www.newgrad-jobs.com/)** (Jobright) — tracks new-grad and university-grad postings with recommended application timing and visa-sponsorship flags per listing. Broader than the GitHub repos, still tech-weighted.
- **NUworks / Handshake** — the one you should not skip. Northeastern's Handshake instance is where employers post reqs *specifically targeted at Northeastern students and alumni*, and the applicant pool is a few hundred people instead of a few thousand. Alumni access policies vary by school and by how recently you graduated, so confirm what you still have. If your access lapsed, contact Northeastern career services and ask what alumni retain. This is the single highest signal-to-noise channel available to you and it costs one email to find out.
- **Built In Boston** and **Wellfound** — already in your Workday file. Both let you filter by experience level; set the filter to 0-2 years and save it as a separate alert from your main search.

# Suggested weekly routine

1. **September and October, weekly:** check Tier 1 UR pages directly. These are the short-window, high-value ones. Set a recurring calendar block.
2. **Monthly:** skim the Simplify GitHub repo for which companies have opened their cycle, then go to those companies' UR pages and search design.
3. **Ongoing:** Simplify alerts + NUworks + Built In Boston 0-2 years filter.
4. **Once, now:** confirm your Northeastern Handshake alumni access.

A realistic expectation: this channel will produce maybe 10 to 20 genuinely
applicable reqs across the whole fall, not 100. That is the point. The
conversion rate on a targeted UR design req where you meet the profile is
worth more than another 100 open-market applications.
